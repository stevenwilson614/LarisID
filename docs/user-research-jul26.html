Do not read the scroll-depth numbers

Clarity reports 44.3% average scroll for A and 99.5% for B. B is a short chat page where full depth is trivially reached — the figure measures page length, not interest. scroll_depth_* also fires on the A arm only, so there is no comparable B measurement to be had. gap

Recommendation — keep both arms running

Session→signup is a tie, B wins on depth, A wins on CTA rate. There is no evidence base for retiring either arm, and the 22 July traffic collapse means recent data is thin. The interesting question is no longer which arm converts — it is why nobody comes back to either.

03 — Before signupWhere anonymous visitors quit

678 Clarity sessions over 30 days, plus server-side page_views since the 20 July deploy. Roughly 59% of sessions are mobile, and zero rage clicks were recorded site-wide — the landing page is not frustrating anyone. It is simply failing to hold them.

The email door that never opens

Inside the auth modal, "Lanjutkan dengan Google" takes 16 clicks and "Daftar dengan Email" takes 14 — near-even intent. Yet every one of the 30 cohort signups arrived through Google, and not a single email signup completed.

A further 9 people opened the signup modal and closed it again. Roughly half of all signup intent on this page is currently discarded. fact

Traffic collapsed after 22 July

04 — After signupWhat the 30 did

All 30 arrived through Google OAuth. Thirteen came from the ab_gpt_jul26 ad campaign, seven from ChatGPT referrals, and the campaign feeds both arms — it points at /, so the random split happens downstream of the ad.

What they were looking for

Onboarding categories skew beauty-first — Kecantikan 10, then Fashion, Bayi & Anak, HP & Gadget and Kamar Mandi at 6 each. Regions are Jakarta-weighted (7) with a real secondary-city tail: Bandung, Surabaya, Surakarta, Semarang, Tasikmalaya, Jepara.

But typed searches tell a different story than the category picker. The logged queries resolve to about a dozen real intents, and most are brand or competitor lookups — Sipolos, Andistro, morinaga, chilkid — or own-market checks like Kaos polos tasikmalaya, typed by a user whose registered region is Tasikmalaya. pattern

In the B arm, 90 chat messages split between canned suggestion chips and genuinely typed prompts. The typed ones are almost uniformly a single request: find me a winner — Produk bakal viral, produk fashion trending kompetisi rendah, Cari skincare terlaris, Produk yang laris 2026. One is a support question asked twice — Gimana caranya masuk link toko — suggesting store-level analysis is wanted but undiscoverable. hypothesis

05 — SynthesisWhat users actually need

Derived from behaviour and from the words users typed, ranked by weight of evidence — not from assumptions about what sellers want.

  1. "Tell me what to sell" — a shortlist, not a feed pattern

    27 of 30 reach Discover and 11 of 30 die there. Typed chat prompts are almost all trending / viral / terlaris / kompetisi rendah. Users are not asking to browse; they are asking to be handed a small set of answers. Strongest finding here — two independent sources agree, behaviour and language.

  2. "Check this specific thing I already have in mind" pattern

    Every typed search is a named brand or a personal niche, often against the user's own city. These are sellers validating an existing hypothesis, not discovering from scratch. Only 6 of 30 typed anything — but of those who did, this is what they did.

  3. "Will this actually make me money?" pattern

    Hitung estimasi profit is a repeatedly-clicked chip, and 8 calculator rows from 6 users show the MLS calculator being opened. The question after "what should I sell" is immediately "what do I earn".

  4. "Let me look at more than three things" fact

    6 of 17 active user-days end at the 3-dive cap, and four users' final action is a limit wall. Three dives is below the number needed to form a judgement when comparing products.

  5. "Sign me in the way I expect" fact

    14 clicks on email signup; zero email signups have ever completed. Users are choosing a door that does not open.

06 — NextFive things to change

Ranked by expected impact over effort.

  1. Remove or fix the email signup path

    It absorbs roughly 47% of auth-modal clicks and converts nobody. Either fix deliverability and confirmation end to end, or demote it behind a "lainnya" link and make Google the single obvious door. The cheapest measurable win available.

  2. Find and kill the two dismiss buttons on /

    They out-click the primary CTA three to one. #sidebar-overlay at rank 6 is the lead. Remove the interruption or defer it until after first scroll, then re-measure CTA click rate.

  3. Restructure the 3-dive daily cap

    Four users' last-ever action is a limit event and 35% of active days hit the ceiling. Test a larger first-day allowance so the first session can reach a decision. Measure the share of user-days ending at the cap, and day-2 return.

  4. Fix the hero, and fix it on mobile first

    A third of pageviews never scroll, and A's largest contentful paint is 6.3 seconds against a roughly 64%-mobile paid audience. B already proves 77/100 is achievable on the same stack.

  5. Close the instrumentation gaps below

    Log /gpt/ pageviews server-side and fire scroll_depth_* on the B arm. Without these, the next A/B read arrives with exactly the same blind spots as this one.

07 — CaveatsWhat this pass could not measure

Confirmed in code and data. Each of these bounds a claim above.

  • /gpt/ pageviews are never written to page_views — _lidLogPageView() runs from js/laris-app.js only. B-arm landing volume exists in Clarity alone.
  • scroll_depth_* fires on arm A only (js/laris-app.js:24339), so B's 99.5% is a page-length artifact.
  • No CTA-click event reaches the database; only Clarity carries cta_signup_click and signup_cta_source.
  • No view-open events for Tools, Akademi, MLS or community — reaching those surfaces is inferred.
  • unlock_modal_shown and deepdive_teaser_click remain at zero site-wide since 10 July. Absence is not evidence of behaviour; these hooks are unverified.
  • gpt_ai_reply badly undercounts — 10 events against 50 assistant messages actually stored. Unusable as a reply-rate metric.
  • deepdive_open (116 events / 25 users) and metered usage_events dives (30 / 10 users) disagree about fourfold. Both are reported as-is rather than reconciled; resolve before either becomes a KPI.
  • seller_status is null for 26 of 30 since the 9 July onboarding cut — pemula versus existing seller cannot be segmented from preferences.
  • Clarity totals include local development traffic: 112 pageviews from https://Electron, localhost and 127.0.0.1.
  • One user has no signup_attribution row, so channel data covers 29 of 30.
  • Instagram sent 21 sessions in 30 days and TikTok 1, but no cohort user carries social attribution — that traffic either bounces or loses its referrer.
Scope limit, stated plainly

The qualitative layer here comes from heatmaps, ranked click elements, dead-click and quick-back rates, and stored chat text — not from watching individual session recordings. No claim above rests on one.

Recording review is the best next step for the two open questions: what those two dismiss buttons actually close, and what happens in the 88 sessions that reached for a CTA and never signed up.
